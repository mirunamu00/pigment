## What to fix

Critic findings that fail the candidate:

1. **major**, componentStates, 1 of 1 sample(s)
   - Principle to fix: When a whole card is a link, put the lift (the -4px translate and shadow.md) on the card's outer bordered box, meaning the anchor itself or the element that wraps it and receives the pointer. Don't put it on an inner element or leave it off. Keep 8px of clearance so no ancestor's overflow clips the shadow. Check it by hovering one card and seeing it move off the page with the offset shadow behind it.
   - What was seen (for understanding only): Linked product cards don't lift on hover. They don't move -4px and get no hard offset shadow, so in both themes a hovered card looks the same as one at rest. The hard lift on linked cards in a grid is one of the system's four signature elements.
   - Evidence: `showcase/runs/hardlift/0.1.0-rc.2/commerce/20261019-123257/screenshots/state-card-hover-desktop-light.png`, `showcase/runs/hardlift/0.1.0-rc.2/commerce/20261019-123257/screenshots/state-card-hover-desktop-dark.png`
2. **major**, signature, 1 of 1 sample(s), caused by the spec
   - Principle to fix: Pink goes to the single most important action in view. Some components bring their own pink button (an empty state, a form, a dialog). When one of them appears where a pink action is already visible, decide which action matters most right now and give the other one lift--ink or lift--ghost, so only one pink action is ever visible. Open menu triggers, selected controls and toggled states use the raised or ink fill, never pink.
   - What was seen (for understanding only): More than one pink action shows in the same viewport. A component's own pink button (the empty-state 'Clear filters', the newsletter Subscribe) appears next to a screen-level pink action (Refresh), and an open menu trigger also turns pink. This breaks the rule that pink is kept for one main action per viewport.
   - Evidence: `showcase/runs/hardlift/0.1.0-rc.2/dashboard/20261019-123257/screenshots/state-empty-filter-desktop-light.png`, `showcase/runs/hardlift/0.1.0-rc.2/dashboard/20261019-123257/screenshots/state-user-menu-open-desktop-light.png`, `showcase/runs/hardlift/0.1.0-rc.2/review-1/sheets/blog--state--search-empty--mobile-light.png`
3. **major**, signature, 1 of 1 sample(s), caused by the spec
   - Principle to fix: Don't draw a generic 'image' glyph (mountains and sun, a picture frame) as a placeholder or an illustration. Where a real photo will be supplied later, the placeholder is a band-color square holding a simple sticker object drawn from the basic shapes (ellipse, rounded rect, tag, coin, star, arrow) that hints at that item's own kind. Where a figure stands for an idea, build an original sticker scene that shows that figure's subject. Two items or figures never share one drawing.
   - What was seen (for understanding only): Every image and figure uses the same generic 'picture' glyph (mountains and sun, or a framed-image icon) on a band-color square. That covers all 11 product tiles, both product detail images, and both article figures under different captions. Nothing tells one item from another, and the figures read as missing images. The spec asks for original sticker objects drawn from basic shapes, and the reference's colorful product wall works because each tile is different.
   - Evidence: `showcase/runs/hardlift/0.1.0-rc.2/blog/20261019-123257/drawings/posts-shipping-a-design-system-d1.png`, `showcase/runs/hardlift/0.1.0-rc.2/review-1/sheets/blog--drawings--posts-release-notes-people-read.png`, `showcase/runs/hardlift/0.1.0-rc.2/review-1/sheets/commerce--drawings--home.png`, `showcase/runs/hardlift/0.1.0-rc.2/review-1/sheets/commerce--drawings--products-canvas-field-tote.png`
