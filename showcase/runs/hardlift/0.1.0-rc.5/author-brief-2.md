1. **blocker**, componentStates, 2 of 3 sample(s), caused by the spec
   - Principle to fix: A toast never covers content or controls. Pin it to a viewport corner outside the content column. Where content reaches that corner (narrow screens, or forms whose actions sit at the bottom-left), either reserve bottom padding on the page equal to the toast height plus space.16, or show the confirmation inline beside the action that triggered it. The toast's own controls are ghost or ink, never pink. After the toast appears, check in each theme and at 390px that the button which triggered it is still fully visible.
   - What was seen (for understanding only): The save-confirmation toast lands on top of the form's action row. At phone width the bottom-pinned toast covers the lower half of both Save and Reset. This is a regression: review 1 of this version showed an inline 'Saved' check that covered nothing.

Place each shape so that its whole travel path, from the start of the drift range to the end, stays in empty hero space or past the hero's edges. Keep shapes below text and controls in the stacking order (lower z-index, pointer-events: none). Under breakpoint.md, either shrink the shapes and move them to the hero's edges, or render them static below the content. Check at 390px, scrolled to the top, the middle and the end of the hero, that every label in the hero is fully visible.
   - What was seen (for understanding only): At phone width, once the page is scrolled, the scroll-bound hero drift moves the coin, star and speech-bubble stickers up over the pink 'Read the featured post' CTA. The keyboard-focus capture at the top of the page shows no overlap, so the overlap depends on scroll position.

3. **blocker**, polish, 1 of 3 sample(s)

4. **major**, componentStates, 1 of 3 sample(s), caused by the spec
   - Principle to fix: A busy button is not a disabled button. It keeps its variant's fill, ink border and full-contrast label, and shows the busy state with the small turning coin, a changed label and aria-busy. The gray disabled recipe applies only on paper or raised surfaces. On a band, pink or any other colored fill, a disabled or busy control keeps its normal fill and border, drops the lift and uses aria-disabled. Every state of a control must meet 4.5:1 against the fill directly behind it.
   - What was seen (for understanding only): While refreshing, the band's action button turns into a borderless pale-gray box with pale-gray 'Refreshing…' text that is barely readable on the teal band, in light and dark. The spec's generic disabled recipe (subtle fill, disabled text, hairline border) conflicts with its own ban on disabled-looking text on a band or colored fill and with the 4.5:1 floor. The other samples keep the ink or paper button with a turning coin.
