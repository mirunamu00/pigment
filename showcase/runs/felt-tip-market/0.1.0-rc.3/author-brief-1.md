What keeps it from the reference's quality:

1. **major gap from the reference**, signature, 1 of 1 sample(s)
   - Principle to fix: Measure the depth in rendered screen pixels, not viewBox units. The solid strip between face and back must be at least 10% of the drawing's rendered height (about 30px on a 300px object) and show along the whole lower edge, not just at the corners. Apply the back's offset outside any rotation, so it shifts straight down on screen. For irregular shapes (garments, bags), draw the back as the whole silhouette shifted down and fill the gap as one solid region; never leave a doubled outline, and never let the back line up with a hem or rim so it reads as decoration. Objects that are thin in real life (sheet, pad, envelope, card, clipboard, ticket) are drawn as thick board: a pad with a page stack, an envelope as a padded mailer, with the same 10–15% strip as a box. In a media box wider than 320px the outer outline is 3px. Before finishing, compare the largest drawing on each screen with the smallest card vignette: if the big one shows less strip, deepen it.
   - What was seen (for understanding only): Large drawings still read as flat outlines. On the item page the main drawing has a back strip only a few pixels deep, or its depth shows as a second hem line. In the heroes, objects that are thin in real life (notepad, envelope, clipboard) have a side strip of about 3–5% of their height. The reference's objects are chunky die-cuts with a deep solid rim at every size, and that weight is what makes them hold their own against the headline. Boxy and round nouns (button, jar, clock, ticket, folder, badge) now meet the recipe, so the gap has narrowed but is still open on the largest and flattest objects. The spec already says 'at least 10%' and 'measure before you finish', and agents still miss it on irregular and flat shapes.
   - Evidence: `showcase/runs/felt-tip-market/0.1.0-rc.3/review-1/crops/commerce--page--products-canvas-field-tote--desktop-light--s1.png`, `showcase/runs/felt-tip-market/0.1.0-rc.3/review-1/crops/commerce--page--products-merino-crew-sweater--desktop-light--s1.png`, `showcase/runs/felt-tip-market/0.1.0-rc.3/review-1/crops/blog--page--home--desktop-light--s1.png`, `showcase/runs/felt-tip-market/0.1.0-rc.3/blog/20261025-031927/drawings/home-d2.png`, `showcase/runs/felt-tip-market/0.1.0-rc.3/review-1/sheets/blog--drawings--about.png`, `showcase/runs/felt-tip-market/0.1.0-rc.3/review-1/crops/dashboard--page--projects--desktop-light--s1.png`, `pipeline/gumroad-study/reference/set/01-desktop.png`
2. **major gap from the reference**, signature, 1 of 1 sample(s)
   - Principle to fix: The hero's action row is always a pair at one shared height (56–64px on desktop): the primary CTA as a large block button in the plate stack (ink in light, pink in dark), and beside it a bordered input that fits the product (search, email, or a lookup), at least as wide as the CTA. If the product has no natural input, make the second element an outline button of the same height. Never leave a lone default-size button under a hero headline. On phones the pair stacks at full width.
   - What was seen (for understanding only): Every front-page hero anchors its action with one compact pink button, about 48px tall. The reference sets a large block CTA next to a bordered input of the same height under the 96px headline, and that pair gives the hero its weight. The spec asks for 'plate-stack CTA + bordered input' in the hero row. No app followed it, so the hero rows look light and generic beside a strong sticker ring.
