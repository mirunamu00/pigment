## What to fix

Quality against the reference, below the bar:

- **signature** 3 (needs 4)

What keeps it from the reference's quality:

1. **major gap from the reference**, signature, 3 of 3 sample(s)
   - Principle to fix: A sticker is a thick object, not an outline. Draw the face. Repeat the same shape 10–15% of the object's height lower as its back, in the same fill and outlined in ink. Join the two with the side silhouette so a solid band of edge shows, then add 1–3 inner strokes (rim, fold, seam). A shape with one outline and a hairline edge is not a sticker. The first screen of a front page (home of a publication, store or product site) is a hero band with a hero-size headline and 4–6 different stickers ringing it, never a compact header band. On a hero, at least half the stickers are 160–240px wide on desktop, and no object is drawn twice. A header-band sticker is at least as tall as the band's title block (never under 96px on desktop or 64px on phones). It sits vertically centred at the band's trailing edge, is cropped by that side only (never by the band's top or bottom seam) and shows at least 60% of itself on every viewport. When the band is too short, shrink the sticker or move it inward. An empty state draws the missing thing in its own silhouette and pose, never a recoloured copy of another sticker on the page.
   - What was seen (for understanding only): The sticker language is still well below the reference in every app, and the earlier verdict's main gap has not closed. The reference's stickers are large die-cut objects, about 200px, with a deep extruded rim, and they shape the composition at the band edges. Here the hero objects are mostly 100–150px flat line drawings with little or no back face. Every product app has a single header-band sticker of about 60–100px, pushed into the band's top corner and cropped by the side and the top seam, so on desktop it reads as a fragment and on phones only a sliver shows. Of the settings stickers, only the billing token has a real side. The empty-cart drawing reuses the header bag, recoloured. One blog sample has no front-page hero at all, only a compact header band with one cut-off object.

2. **major gap from the reference**, referenceFidelity, 3 of 3 sample(s)
   - The spec's words that lead to it: Dark tokens: `color.surface.default` | `#242423`; `color.surface.raised` | `#000000`; Status pill: `color.surface.raised`, `border.width.hair` `color.border.default`
   - Principle to fix: Dark tokens: the page ground `color.surface.default` is #000000 and raised boxes `color.surface.raised` are one step lighter, #242423, edged with the 35% white hairline. Depth then reads as it does in light mode, with white boxes on paper. The top bar and footer stay black fields. A toast, menu or dialog on a black field stays on the raised tone and keeps a visible hard shadow. Anything placed on a colour band (status pill, outline button, chip) keeps its light-mode white fill and ink text in both modes. A sticker keeps its ink outline in both modes, so it sits on a band or raised field, never directly on black.
   - What was seen (for understanding only): Dark mode reverses the reference's depth on every screen of every app. The page ground is dark grey (#242423) and every card, panel, table, menu, dialog, input and empty box is pure black, where the reference has a black ground with boxes a step lighter. Panels sink instead of rising. The black top bar and footer merge with the panels. Hard shadows (35% white) barely show, so menus, dialogs and the hover lift lose their picked-up depth. Dashed frames fade, and an ink-outlined sticker inside a black box loses its outline. In the same way, the status pill on a colour band turns black in dark because it is filled with `color.surface.raised`. The spec's own dark tokens set this up, and it has not changed since the earlier review.
   - Evidence: `showcase/runs/felt-tip-market/0.1.0-rc.1/review-2/crops/dashboard--page--home--desktop-dark--s2.png`, `showcase/runs/felt-tip-market/0.1.0-rc.1/review-2/crops/commerce--page--home--desktop-dark--s1.png`, `showcase/runs/felt-tip-market/0.1.0-rc.1/review-2/crops/commerce--page--cart--desktop-dark--s1.png`, `showcase/runs/felt-tip-market/0.1.0-rc.1/review-2/crops/dashboard--state--user-menu-open--desktop-dark--s3.png`, `showcase/runs/felt-tip-market/0.1.0-rc.1/review-2/sheets/dashboard--page--projects-apollo-migration--desktop-dark.png`, `pipeline/gumroad-study/reference/set/11-desktop.png`
3. **major gap from the reference**, polish, 3 of 3 sample(s)
   - Principle to fix: When an item has no image of its own (a cover, a product photo, a figure), never draw a generic picture glyph (sun and mountains, a landscape, a photo icon) and never fill the box grey. Do one of two things. Draw the item's own subject as a vignette in the sticker recipe: a band-colour ground with 1–3 flat ink-outlined objects named by the item's topic or kind, different for each item. Or show a `color.surface.raised` box with a 1px dashed ink border and a small solid ink glyph of the item's type.
   - What was seen (for understanding only): Content without its own image falls back to a generic picture glyph (sun and mountains, or a photo icon), usually on a flat grey fill. Every product card and product page in commerce does this, and so does every article figure in the blog, where two different subjects share one identical glyph. The reference never shows stock glyphs or grey. Empty media is white with a dashed ink line and a small solid glyph of the thing, and every drawing is a distinct ink-outlined object. Here it is the most visible generic element on the content-heavy screens.
