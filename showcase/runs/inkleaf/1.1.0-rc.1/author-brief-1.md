   - Principle to fix: Split the missing-image rule by what the image is for. (1) An image that stands for a thing (an item, a person's object, the subject of a page) becomes that thing's own drawing, on a plate or drawing stage. (2) An image that stands for information (a diagram, a screenshot, a comparison, a chart; usually it has a caption that describes structure or data) becomes a ghosted UI preview or a simple diagram on a stage, built from the system's own surfaces (raised cards, hairlines, skeleton bars), never a pictogram. In Verification, add: 'Read each figure's caption: the figure must show what the caption describes; a caption about a layout, list, board or comparison never sits under a single object drawing.'
   - What was seen (for understanding only): Regression from 1.0.0, caused by the update's new long-form rule. In-article figures that stand for a diagram or a screenshot are now replaced by one large pictogram of a loosely related object, and the drawing no longer shows what its caption says. A caption about an inventory board of grouped components sits under a single sewing button. A caption about one release written for two audiences sits under a pencil. Both are full text-column plates holding a 416px drawing in the middle of the prose. In 1.0.0 the same figures were ghosted UI previews on a stage: a grid of grouped tiles, and two side-by-side panels. Those matched their captions and followed the reference's habit of putting UI on stages. The cause is the new line in Layout adaptation: 'An image without a photo becomes a drawing of the page's or item's own subject (Imagery), never removed.' It treats every missing image as an item photo. The rc.3 distinction ('diagrams and screenshots sit on a stage') is gone.
